# Agent 7 — LAPPL as a Political Spender, 2013–2026

## Findings (chronological)

### 2013 — Mayor: Wendy Greuel vs. Eric Garcetti
- **Source:** HuffPost, "Wendy Greuel's Police Union Donations Reach $1.4 Million After Major Infusion," May 9, 2013 — https://www.huffpost.com/2013/05/09/wendy-greuel-police-union-donations-_n_3245240.html
- **Outcome:** LOST (per source — see LA Times item below)
- **What it says:** LAPPL, representing about 10,000 officers, had by May 9, 2013 spent roughly $1.4 million backing Wendy Greuel for mayor, including an $850,000 independent-expenditure contribution described as "the largest single contribution from one source during the campaign." The article also flagged a potential conflict of interest: LAPPL's labor contract was due for mayoral approval/renewal the following year, and the union had already endorsed Greuel earlier in 2013.
- **Verbatim quote:** "The Los Angeles Police Protective League, which represents about 10,000 police officers, has now spent roughly $1.4 million to back Greuel." — HuffPost, https://www.huffpost.com/2013/05/09/wendy-greuel-police-union-donations-_n_3245240.html

### 2013 — Election result and union-spending framing
- **Source:** Los Angeles Times (James Rainey and Seema Mehta), "Elections deal a blow to L.A. unions," May 23, 2013 (archived reprint) — https://catherwood.library.cornell.edu/wit/elections-deal-a-blow-to-l-a-unions
- **Outcome:** LOST — Garcetti won the runoff; Greuel, backed by LAPPL and roughly 30 unions/locals, lost.
- **What it says:** The Times covered Greuel's defeat as a setback for organized labor generally — she had "the support of about 30 unions and locals, who made hefty financial contributions to her campaign," LAPPL's $850,000+ among the largest of those. (Full body text of the archived reprint was truncated to this summary; only the excerpt above was retrievable.)
- **Verbatim quote:** "The defeat of Wendy Greuel in Tuesday's mayoral runoff came as a disappointment to organized labor." — LA Times/Rainey & Mehta, https://catherwood.library.cornell.edu/wit/elections-deal-a-blow-to-l-a-unions

### 2017 — Charter Amendment C (LAPD disciplinary panels)
- **Source:** Ballotpedia, "Los Angeles, California, Civilian Review of Police Disciplinary Matters, Measure C (May 2017)" — https://ballotpedia.org/Los_Angeles,_California,_Civilian_Review_of_Police_Disciplinary_Matters,_Measure_C_(May_2017)
- **Outcome:** PASSED — 57.14% of the vote.
- **What it says:** Charter Amendment C let LAPD officers facing serious-misconduct discipline choose an all-civilian three-member Board of Rights instead of the standard panel of two command officers and one civilian. It was placed on the ballot by the City Council with support from Mayor Eric Garcetti, Council President Herb Wesson, and LAPPL, which had long sought the change, arguing the mixed panels were unfair to officers.
- **Verbatim quote:** (see next entry for direct sourced criticism)

### 2017 — Criticism that Amendment C would ease, not toughen, discipline
- **Source:** ACLU of Southern California press release, "Civil Rights Groups Call on City Leaders for Comprehensive Review of Police Discipline after Passage of Charter Amendment C," May 17, 2017 — https://www.aclusocal.org/press-releases/civil-rights-groups-call-city-leaders-comprehensive-review-police-discipline-after/
- **Outcome:** PASSED (measure), but the release documents organized opposition from ACLU SoCal and Black Lives Matter LA.
- **What it says:** ACLU SoCal argued Amendment C would weaken accountability rather than strengthen it, citing city data that civilian Board of Rights panelists were "consistently more lenient on officers" and "reject the chief of police's recommendation to fire officers more than half the time." The release states LAPPL and allied interests "outspent opposition groups 50-to-1," spending over $1.25 million on ads that framed the measure as expanding civilian oversight.
- **Verbatim quotes:**
  - "[Amendment C would result in] weakening discipline and accountability at LAPD" — Peter Bibring, ACLU SoCal director of police practices, https://www.aclusocal.org/press-releases/civil-rights-groups-call-city-leaders-comprehensive-review-police-discipline-after/
  - "Most voters who voted by mail were duped by the police union." — Melina Abdullah, Black Lives Matter LA, same source

### 2022 — Mayor: $4M opposing Karen Bass, backing Rick Caruso
- **Source:** ABC7, "LA mayor's race: Police union's new ads blast Karen Bass for missing votes in Congress," May 26, 2022 — https://abc7.com/post/los-angeles-police-protective-league-new-tv-ads-congresswoman-karen-bass-rick-caruso-political-ad/11895088/
- **Outcome:** WON — Bass ultimately won the 2022 mayor's race despite LAPPL's opposition (per subsequent LAist/LA Public Press coverage establishing Bass took office).
- **What it says:** LAPPL, funded by dues from roughly 10,000 members, had spent nearly $3.5 million on an anti-Bass campaign (two TV ads plus mail) as of late May 2022, and endorsed Bass's opponent Rick Caruso. LAPPL vice president Jerretta Sandoz said of Caruso's independence from the union: "Rick Caruso has not funneled a dime to union." Bass's campaign disputed the union's claims and criticized the spending.
- **Verbatim quotes:**
  - "The Police Protective League has spent $4 million lying about Congresswoman Bass' record." — Bass campaign statement, https://abc7.com/post/los-angeles-police-protective-league-new-tv-ads-congresswoman-karen-bass-rick-caruso-political-ad/11895088/
  - "Congresswoman Bass has cast more than 18,000 votes in Congress, while Rick Caruso neglected his duty to keep people safe by abandoning 40% of his Police Commission meetings." — Bass campaign statement, same source
  - Bass called the union's ad campaign a "waste of money, and it's not good for Los Angeles." — same source

### 2022 — City Council District 11: LAPPL backs Traci Park
- **Source:** Yahoo News (reprint), "Traci Park elected in Westside district, putting a record 6 women on L.A. City Council" — https://news.yahoo.com/traci-park-elected-westside-district-014253208.html
- **Outcome:** WON — Traci Park defeated progressive attorney Erin Darling 52%–48% in the November 8, 2022 general election.
- **What it says:** Coverage of the race described Park's narrow win as made possible in part by "an unprecedented flood of real estate and police union money," referencing LAPPL's "Residents for Safer and Cleaner Neighborhoods Supporting Traci Park" independent expenditure committee, which reporting elsewhere put at over $500,000 in spending.
- **Verbatim quote:** Not independently confirmed verbatim from primary text in this pass; reporting characterization only (see "Could Not Find").

### 2024 — City Council District 4: LAPPL joins coalition to defeat Nithya Raman
- **Source:** LAist, "Real estate mogul, others pour $1M to defeat progressive LA city councilmember Nithya Raman in 2024 election" (Frank Stoltze), February 13, 2024 — https://laist.com/brief/news/politics/real-estate-mogul-others-pour-1m-to-defeat-progressive-la-city-councilmember-nithya-raman-in-2024-election
- **Outcome:** UNRESOLVED as of this article (pre-primary); see next entry for result.
- **What it says:** Over $1 million was raised through independent expenditure committees to defeat Raman ahead of the March 5, 2024 primary. Contributors included Douglas Emmett Management ($400,000), LAPPL ($164,000 cited in this specific committee), and the firefighters' union (over $300,000). The piece frames the funding as coming "from a powerful real estate mogul and the labor union that represents Los Angeles police officers."
- **Verbatim quote:** "Special interests have poured more than $1 million into a campaign to defeat L.A. City Councilmember Nithya Raman." — Frank Stoltze, LAist, https://laist.com/brief/news/politics/real-estate-mogul-others-pour-1m-to-defeat-progressive-la-city-councilmember-nithya-raman-in-2024-election

### 2024 — City Council District 4: Raman wins outright, survives opposition spending
- **Source:** AsAmNews, "Los Angeles City Councilmember Nithya Raman secures reelection," March 15, 2024 — https://asamnews.com/2024/03/15/la-election-councilmember-government-politician-lapd-homelessness-crime-voters/ ; corroborated by ABC7, "Los Angeles City Councilmember Nithya Raman wins re-election to District 4 seat" — https://abc7.com/post/los-angeles-city-councilwoman-nithya-raman-wins-reelection/14525672/
- **Outcome:** WON — Raman won outright in the March 2024 primary with 50.6% of the vote (over challenger Ethan Weaver's 38.6% and Levon Baronian's 10.7%), avoiding a November runoff despite the LAPD-union-backed opposition spending on Weaver.
- **What it says:** Raman's leading opponent, deputy city attorney Ethan Weaver, was backed by LAPD-union and real-estate money (the same coalition described in the LAist item above). Raman surpassed 50%, which reporting attributed to voters rejecting the heavy outside spending against her.
- **Verbatim quote:** Raman said the results made her "proud to be an Angeleno," attributing it to voters rejecting the heavy spending by Weaver and the unions backing him. — ABC7, https://abc7.com/post/los-angeles-city-councilwoman-nithya-raman-wins-reelection/14525672/

- **Source:** LAist, "Airbnb, the LAPD union, a landlord giant: Where the money's coming from in LA's race for mayor," August 5, 2026 — https://laist.com/news/politics/bass-campaign-spending-finance-raman-mayor

The article does not itself discuss or explain the reversal from LAPPL's 2022 anti-Bass spending; that context (LAPPL opposed Bass in 2022, backs her in 2026) was found in a separate, companion piece — see next entry.
- **Verbatim quote:** "The largest spender so far is the LAPD officers' union, which reported spending $1.2 million on ads against Raman — who made the runoff — and progressive community organizer Rae Huang, who didn't." — LAist, https://laist.com/news/politics/bass-campaign-spending-finance-raman-mayor

### 2026 — Reporting that explicitly addresses the LAPPL reversal (opposed Bass in 2022, now backs her)
- **Source:** AOL/Yahoo reprint of wire-service coverage, "Nithya Raman whines about LA police union attack ads despite anti-police voting history," May 1, 2026 — https://www.aol.com/news/nithya-raman-whines-la-police-190601230.html (also mirrored at https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/nithya-raman-whines-la-police-190601763.html)
- **Outcome:** UNRESOLVED (race ongoing per source)
- **What it says:** The article states the police union was Bass's "foe when she ran for office in 2022, backing her opponent Rick Caruso," but "she's running for re-election in 2026 — and this time she has the union's support" — explicitly naming the reversal. It also lays out the policy backstory the article ties to the current LAPPL-Raman conflict: Raman opposed the 2023 LAPD contract that raised officer pay, voted against a 2024 budget with $100M+ in added police funding, backed halving police hiring in 2025, and voted against restoring hiring to 410 officers in early 2026. In a campaign video, Raman called LAPPL "a powerful political force pushing back against her campaign" and said the union spent "hundreds of thousands of dollars in attack ads against her" (this 2026 figure is in addition to / consistent with the $1.2 million total reported by LAist above).
- **Verbatim quote:** "[LAPPL was Bass's] foe when she ran for office in 2022, backing her opponent Rick Caruso, but she's running for re-election in 2026 — and this time she has the union's support." — AOL/wire copy, https://www.aol.com/news/nithya-raman-whines-la-police-190601230.html
