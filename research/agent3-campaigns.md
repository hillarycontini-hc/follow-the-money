### Rick Caruso vs. Karen Bass — Mayor, 2022
- **Source:** PBS SoCal, "How Bass vs. Caruso for L.A. Mayor Became the Most Expensive Race in Los Angeles History," (analysis piece, 2022 cycle) — https://www.pbssocal.org/news-community/how-bass-vs-caruso-for-l-a-mayor-became-the-most-expensive-race-in-los-angeles-history
- **What it says:** Rick Caruso self-funded almost all of his campaign, spending $67.6 million in the general election period (about $34M per the city Ethics Commission in another framing) versus Karen Bass's $5.2 million, funded largely by a personal loan from Caruso. Caruso spent $23.9 million on TV/radio/digital ads — about 85% of all ad spending in the race — while Bass and allied groups spent under $2 million on ads combined; Bass benefited from $7.6 million in independent expenditures. Despite outspending Bass roughly 13-to-1, Caruso lost, paying $160.42 per vote received (combined primary+general: $338.42/vote) versus Bass's $21.94/vote (combined). Bass was sworn in as LA's 43rd mayor December 12, 2022.
- **Verbatim quote:** Article states Caruso "self-finance[d] almost all of his campaign" — PBS SoCal, https://www.pbssocal.org/news-community/how-bass-vs-caruso-for-l-a-mayor-became-the-most-expensive-race-in-los-angeles-history

### José Huizar — Council District 14, corruption case (post-2015 term)
- **Source:** U.S. Attorney's Office, Central District of California (DOJ), "Former Los Angeles Politician Jose Huizar Sentenced to 13 Years in Federal Prison" — https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/former-los-angeles-politician-jose-huizar-sentenced-13-years-federal-prison
- **What it says:** Huizar, who represented CD-14 (downtown LA and surrounding communities) from 2005 until his 2020 resignation, was sentenced to 13 years in federal prison after pleading guilty to racketeering conspiracy (RICO) and tax evasion. Prosecutors said Huizar led the "CD-14 Enterprise," a pay-to-play scheme in which he used his office to give favorable treatment to real estate developers in exchange for at least $1.5 million in cash and other benefits, including campaign donations. Judge John F. Walter ordered $443,905 restitution to the City of Los Angeles and $38,792 to the IRS.

### Mark Ridley-Thomas — Council District 10, 2020 (post-election corruption case)
- **Source:** U.S. Attorney's Office, Central District of California (DOJ) sentencing release, and ABC7 Los Angeles, "Former LA City Councilman Mark Ridley-Thomas sentenced to more than 3 years in prison in corruption case" — https://abc7.com/mark-ridley-thomas-sentencing-corruption-case-los-angeles-city-council/13710931/ ; DOJ: https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/mark-ridley-thomas-sentenced-3-12-years-prison-corruptly-securing-benefits-son-school
- **What it says:** Ridley-Thomas was convicted March 30 (2023) on one count each of conspiracy, bribery, and honest services mail fraud, plus four counts of honest services wire fraud, for helping USC win LA County contracts in exchange for funneling $100,000 from his ballot committee through USC into his son's nonprofit. On Aug. 28, 2023, U.S. District Judge Dale Fischer sentenced him to 3.5 years in prison and a $30,000 fine. The Ninth Circuit later affirmed the bribery-related convictions, rejecting his argument that routing campaign funds through USC to his son's charity did not violate federal law.

### Kevin de León vs. Ysabel Jurado — Council District 14, 2024
- **Source:** LAist, "Kevin de León concedes District 14 race to Ysabel Jurado," November 8, 2024 — https://laist.com/news/politics/2024-election-california-general-los-angeles-city-council-district-14-results-analysis
- **What it says:** De León conceded on the evening of Friday, Nov. 8, 2024, to tenants-rights attorney Ysabel Jurado, who won by nearly 13 points and became the first person of Filipino descent elected to the LA City Council. The race was shadowed by the 2022 leaked-audio scandal in which de León was recorded making racist remarks alongside Nury Martinez and Gil Cedillo; de León refused to resign at the time. On spending: outside independent expenditures opposing Jurado topped $260,000 (mostly from the LA Police Protective League's IE committee), Jurado raised roughly $587,000 including from the California Working Families Party and Immigrant Power PAC, and de León drew over $757,000 in outside spending support.
- **Verbatim quote:** "I owned it, and I apologized. But I didn't quit on you because that was too much at stake." — Kevin de León, Election Night remarks, per LAist, https://laist.com/news/politics/2024-election-california-general-los-angeles-city-council-district-14-results-analysis
- **Verbatim quote:** "In the first hundred days, we're going to go and start town halls because we want to know what the voters really care about" — Ysabel Jurado, Election Night event, per LAist, same URL.

### Karen Bass — Mayor, 2026 (reelection campaign)
- **Source:** NBC Los Angeles, "Karen Bass outpaces Nithya Raman in campaign fundraising," 2026 — https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/la-mayor-campaign-fundraising/3924784/
- **What it says:** Bass raised more than $3.7 million to challenger Councilmember Nithya Raman's more than $1.1 million (in one reporting window Raman reported $170,000 in contributions vs. Bass's $510,000). Separately, a Bass campaign video posted to her official X account on Aug. 18, 2026 promoted LA's taxpayer-funded "Super Match" public financing program, which matches qualifying resident donations at 6-to-1 with public money; the video went viral and drew criticism over the ethics/optics of touting taxpayer-funded campaign cash while the city faces budget strain. The program is legal, administered by the LA City Ethics Commission under the city charter, and open to any candidate who opts in and accepts spending limits. The 2026 mayoral primary is set for June 2, 2026, with a runoff (if needed) on November 3, 2026.

### Nury Martinez / Gil Cedillo / Kevin de León — Leaked audio scandal (2022), affecting CD1 and CD14 races
- **Source:** CBS News, "Los Angeles City Council President Nury Martinez faces uproar over racist remarks," 2022 — https://www.cbsnews.com/news/nury-martinez-los-angeles-city-council-president-racist-remarks-uproar
- **What it says:** The LA Times first reported on a secretly recorded October 2021 conversation among then-Council President Nury Martinez, Councilmembers Gil Cedillo and Kevin de León, and LA County Federation of Labor President Ron Herrera discussing redistricting; the recording leaked and was published in 2022. Martinez resigned as Council President; she and Herrera later resigned their positions entirely. Cedillo did not resign but lost his District 1 seat to Eunisses Hernandez in the 2022 race, stepping down that November. De León refused calls to resign. The scandal became a defining backdrop for Cedillo's 2022 CD1 loss and shadowed de León's unsuccessful 2024 reelection bid against Ysabel Jurado.
- **Verbatim quote:** Martinez referred to Councilman Mike Bonin's Black two-year-old son as "Parece changuito" (translated: "that little monkey") — per CBS News, https://www.cbsnews.com/news/nury-martinez-los-angeles-city-council-president-racist-remarks-uproar
- **Verbatim quote:** Mike Bonin condemned the conversation for its "vulgar anti-Black sentiment" — per CBS News, same URL.

### Spencer Pratt — Mayor, 2026
- **Source:** CNBC, "MTV star Spencer Pratt gaining in Los Angeles mayoral race against Karen Bass," May 28, 2026 — https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/28/spencer-pratt-los-angeles-mayor-race-karen-bass-primary-democrats.html
- **What it says:** Former reality-TV personality Spencer Pratt (of MTV's "The Hills") entered the 2026 LA mayoral race after his Pacific Palisades home burned in the January 2025 wildfires; he announced his candidacy on the fire's one-year anniversary at a "They Let Us Burn" rally in Palisades Village attended by about 1,000 demonstrators. His platform centers on homelessness, crime, fire preparedness and cost-of-doing-business issues, including more funding for LAPD and cuts to homelessness funding. CNBC reported Pratt polling at 22%, trailing Bass and Raman but a notable third-place showing. Primary: June 2, 2026; general (if needed): November 3, 2026.
