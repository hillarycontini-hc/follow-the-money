## Findings

### Finding 1: Bass vastly outraised Raman via independent expenditure committees in 2026 mayor's race
- **Source:** LAist, "Airbnb, the LAPD union, a landlord giant: Where the money's coming from in LA's race for mayor," Nick Gerda and Maloy Moore, August 5, 2026 — https://laist.com/news/politics/bass-campaign-spending-finance-raman-mayor
- **What it says:** Money supporting Mayor Karen Bass — combining direct donations, public matching funds, and outside spending — outpaced money backing Councilmember Nithya Raman by roughly 3 to 1. The gap is far starker for independent expenditure (IE) committees specifically, which favored Bass 36-to-1. The LAPD officers' union was the largest single IE spender, reporting $1.2 million against Raman and progressive organizer Rae Huang.
- **Verbatim quote:** "Those money flows from independent expenditure committees have backed Bass 36-to-1, compared to Raman." — LAist, same URL. Also: "State law allows individual donors to spend unlimited amounts on elections through IEs, as long as it's not coordinated with the candidates' campaigns." — same article, same URL.

### Finding 2: IE money is structured to be hard for the public to track
- **Source:** LAist, "Airbnb, the LAPD union, a landlord giant: Where the money's coming from in LA's race for mayor," Nick Gerda and Maloy Moore, August 5, 2026 — https://laist.com/news/politics/bass-campaign-spending-finance-raman-mayor
- **What it says:** The article explains that IE committee money frequently passes through multiple intermediary committees that pool funds meant for several different candidate races before it is spent on election ads, obscuring the money's original source from the public.
- **Verbatim quote:** "That IE money often flows through multiple committees — many of which combine money meant for candidates in multiple different races — on its way to election ads, which can make the money hard for the public to easily track." — LAist, same URL.

### Finding 3: LAist guide on how to trace campaign money in LA local races
- **Source:** LAist, "How to follow the money in local races this election season," Brianna Lee, August 26, 2024 — https://laist.com/news/politics/los-angeles-campaign-finance-follow-money-election-season
- **What it says:** A explainer piece walking readers through how to evaluate LA-area candidate fundraising: checking outside/independent spending, the ratio of small-dollar donors to large donors, and whether contributions originate from within a candidate's own district or from outside interests.
- **Verbatim quote:** "Let's say 40% of a candidate's money is not even coming from their district, it's coming from someplace else." — LAist, same URL. Also: "It's important to just be aware of who is interested in electing somebody or getting a ballot measure passed." — same article, same URL.

### Finding 4: Jose Huizar sentenced to 13 years for $1.5 million pay-to-play scheme
- **Source:** U.S. Attorney's Office, Central District of California (Department of Justice press release), "Former Los Angeles Politician Jose Huizar Sentenced to 13 Years in Federal Prison," reported via multiple outlets including CBS News Los Angeles, January 2024 — https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/former-los-angeles-politician-jose-huizar-sentenced-13-years-federal-prison (official DOJ source; corroborating detail via https://www.cbsnews.com/losangeles/news/former-la-city-councilman-huizar-sentenced-to-13-years-in-prison/)
- **What it says:** Former LA City Councilman Jose Huizar, who chaired the council's Planning and Land Use Management Committee and represented District 14 (downtown LA) from 2005 until resigning in 2020, was sentenced by U.S. District Judge John F. Walter to 13 years in prison after pleading guilty to racketeering conspiracy and tax evasion. The scheme, running 2013–2017, involved roughly $1.5 million in cash bribes, gambling chips, prostitution services, political contributions, private jet flights, luxury hotel stays and other benefits exchanged for help approving downtown high-rise development projects. Huizar was ordered to pay $443,905 restitution to the City of LA and $38,792 to the IRS.
- **Verbatim quote:** "One of the most wide-ranging and brazen public corruption cases uncovered in this district." — U.S. Attorney Martin Estrada, quoted via CBS News Los Angeles coverage of the DOJ announcement, https://www.cbsnews.com/losangeles/news/former-la-city-councilman-huizar-sentenced-to-13-years-in-prison/. Also: "Mr. Huizar's actions, to include accepting a staggering amount of bribe money and lavish gifts, eroded the trust in the office he held for many years." — FBI Assistant Director Donald Alway, same source.

### Finding 5: Los Angeles becomes first US city to ban campaign contributions from real estate developers
- **Source:** The Real Deal, "LA becomes 1st city to enact ban on developer money," December 4, 2019 (headline/date per search result; full article text could not be fetched due to 403 error) — https://therealdeal.com/la/2019/12/04/la-becomes-1st-city-to-enact-ban-on-developer-money/
- **What it says:** On December 4, 2019, the LA City Council voted unanimously to bar people and companies seeking discretionary approval for real estate development projects — plus their corporate executives and investors owning 20%+ stakes — from donating to candidates for mayor, city council, or city attorney while an application is pending and for up to 12 months after approval. The ordinance was introduced after the November 2018 FBI raids on then-Councilman Jose Huizar's home and office. Ethics Commission staff described it as the first such developer-contribution ban by any U.S. jurisdiction.
