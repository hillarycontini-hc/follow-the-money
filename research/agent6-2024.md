# Agent 6 — 2024 LA Cycle: Endorsements & Coverage

## Findings

### CD14 — Ysabel Jurado
- **Source:** California DSA (DSA-LA member org), "Ysabel," Oct 15 [2024] — https://www.californiadsa.org/news/ysabel-october-2024
- **Endorsed/supported by:** DSA-LA (endorsed "early on in the crowded primary"); following the primary, Jurado also secured the LA County Federation of Labor and "around 20 local unions including SEIU (2015, 721, CIR and more), UAW Region 6, and Public Defenders Local 148."
- **What it says:** DSA-LA backed Jurado from the start of the crowded primary because "she had been an active member of DSA and she was running on an unabashedly progressive platform." DSA-LA volunteers knocked almost 9,000 doors while the broader campaign knocked over 80,000. After Jurado advanced to the runoff against incumbent Kevin de León, the LA County Federation of Labor and roughly 20 local unions lined up behind her.
- **Verbatim quote:** "She had been an active member of DSA and she was running on an unabashedly progressive platform." — California DSA, https://www.californiadsa.org/news/ysabel-october-2024

### CD14 — Miguel Santiago (primary) / Ysabel Jurado (LA Times shift)
- **Source:** Los Angeles Times editorial board, "The Times' recommendation for Los Angeles City Council District 14," Jan. 26, 2024 (mirrored) — https://www.yahoo.com/news/times-recommendation-los-angeles-city-193827334.html
- **Endorsed/supported by:** LA Times editorial board endorsed Assemblymember Miguel Santiago in the March primary.
- **What it says:** The board criticized incumbent Kevin de León for refusing to resign after the racist leaked-audio scandal and for poor constituent service. It called Santiago "the best person for the job" among seven candidates trying to oust de León, citing nearly a decade of legislative experience on housing, homelessness and environmental justice. It described runner-up Ysabel Jurado as "a strong candidate" but backed Santiago in the primary. (Note: this fetch only surfaced the primary-round recommendation; a distinct Times runoff endorsement of Jurado could not be independently confirmed — see Could Not Find.)
- **Verbatim quote:** "Of the seven candidates seeking to oust De León, state Assemblymember Miguel Santiago stands out as the best person for the job." — LA Times editorial board, Jan. 26, 2024, https://www.yahoo.com/news/times-recommendation-los-angeles-city-193827334.html

### CD14 — Kevin de León concession / Ysabel Jurado victory (Nov. 5, 2024 general)
- **Source:** LAist, "Kevin de León concedes District 14 race to Ysabel Jurado," Nov. 8, 2024 — https://laist.com/news/politics/2024-election-california-general-los-angeles-city-council-district-14-results-analysis
- **What it says:** De León conceded to Jurado following the November general election, referencing the fallout from the 2022 leaked-audio scandal that had defined his final term. Jurado's statement focused on planning post-election town halls to hear from voters.
- **Verbatim quote:** De León — "I owned it, and I apologized." / "Even in the face of intense, relentless, scrutiny and pressure, we didn't flinch." Jurado — "In the first hundred days, we're going to go and start town halls because we want to know what the voters really care about." / "The outcome didn't matter to me — what we built, what we've done, was something worth noting as itself." — LAist, Nov. 8, 2024, https://laist.com/news/politics/2024-election-california-general-los-angeles-city-council-district-14-results-analysis

### CD4 — Nithya Raman (opposition money)
- **Source:** LAist, "Real Estate Mogul, Others Pour $1M To Defeat Progressive LA City Councilmember Nithya Raman In 2024 Election," Feb. 13, 2024 — https://laist.com/news/politics/real-estate-mogul-others-pour-1m-to-defeat-progressive-la-city-councilmember-nithya-raman-in-2024-election
- **Endorsed/supported by (opposition funders):** Douglas Emmett Management (real-estate firm owned by Dan Emmett) — $400,000; Los Angeles Police Protective League — $164,000; also "significant" (unspecified) contributions from the firefighters' union, the Hollywood Chamber of Commerce, and Kilroy Realty.
- **What it says:** An independent-expenditure effort backing Raman's opponent Ethan Weaver raised "more than $1 million." The article ties Douglas Emmett's $400K to the company's ongoing legal fight to evict tenants from 700+ units at its Barrington Plaza high-rise in Westwood, which Raman (chair of the Council's Homelessness and Housing Committee) had opposed. Douglas Emmett declined to explain the contribution's purpose.
- **Verbatim quote:** "While we are committed to transparency, we do not discuss the specific reasons for our contributions." — Douglas Emmett spokesperson, LAist, Feb. 13, 2024, https://laist.com/news/politics/real-estate-mogul-others-pour-1m-to-defeat-progressive-la-city-councilmember-nithya-raman-in-2024-election

### LAUSD District 3 — Dan Chang vs. Scott Schmerelson
- **Source:** The 74, "Union-Backed Incumbent Prevails in High-Stakes L.A. School Board Race" — https://www.the74million.org/article/union-backed-incumbent-prevails-in-high-stakes-la-school-board-race/ (direct fetch blocked; content below drawn from search-result summary of this article)
- **Endorsed/supported by:** United Teachers Los Angeles (UTLA) endorsed incumbent Scott Schmerelson and, per the article, "put in virtually all of the $2.4 million in independent spending on Schmerelson's behalf." California Charter Schools Association Advocates (CCSA) endorsed challenger Dan Chang.
- **What it says:** Schmerelson defeated charter-backed challenger Dan Chang 52%–48% in the Nov. 5 general. The race carried added stakes because it could have flipped LAUSD's 4–3 union-aligned board majority. Chang, a math teacher who had helped found charter schools, supported charter co-locations on traditional campuses; Schmerelson favored limiting charter access to space in high-needs communities.
- **Verbatim quote:** Not captured directly (page blocked); paraphrase only — see note above.
