# Agent 2 — Academic Research on Campaign Spending & Contributions

## Findings

### Finding 1: Campaign contributions cause congressional offices to grant more access to donors — a randomized field experiment
- **Citation:** Joshua L. Kalla and David E. Broockman, "Campaign Contributions Facilitate Access to Congressional Officials: A Randomized Field Experiment," *American Journal of Political Science*, 60(3), 2016, pp. 545–558 — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ajps.12180 (abstract also mirrored at https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:amposc:v:60:y:2016:i:3:p:545-558)
- **Geography:** US-national (US House offices)
- **What it finds:** In a randomized field experiment, a political organization tried to schedule meetings between 191 congressional offices and its members who were also donors, randomly varying whether the office was told the constituent had contributed to campaigns. Senior policymakers made themselves available 3–4x more often when told the person was a donor, providing causal (not merely correlational) evidence that money buys access to officials, distinct from buying votes.
- **Verbatim quote:** "Concern that donations to political campaigns secure preferential treatment from policy makers has long occupied judges, scholars, and the public. However, the effects of contributions on policy makers' behavior are notoriously difficult to assess. We present the first randomized field experiment on the topic... When informed prospective attendees were political donors, senior policy makers made themselves available between three and four times more often. These findings underscore concerns about the Supreme Court's recent decisions deregulating campaign finance." — Abstract, https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:amposc:v:60:y:2016:i:3:p:545-558

### Finding 2: Donors shape which issues legislators talk about on the floor, even where vote-based studies find little effect
- **Citation:** Pranav Goel, Nikolay Malkin, SoRelle W. Gaynor, Nebojsa Jojic, Kristina Miler, and Philip Resnik, "Donor activity is associated with US legislators' attention to political issues," *PLOS ONE*, 2023 — https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0291169
- **Geography:** US-national (Congress)
- **What it finds:** Instead of looking for a link between donations and roll-call votes (where prior literature is mixed/weak), the authors match donor issue-priorities to legislators' floor speeches and find a robust statistical association — legislators talk more about the issues their donors care about, suggesting money shapes legislative attention/agenda-setting even when it doesn't show up in votes.
- **Verbatim quote:** "Campaign contributions are a staple of congressional life. Yet, the search for tangible effects of congressional donations often focuses on the association between contributions and votes on congressional bills. We present an alternative approach by considering the relationship between money and legislators' speech... Ultimately, we find a robust relationship between donors and speech, indicating a more pervasive role of money in politics than previously assumed." — Abstract, https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0291169

### Finding 3: Real-estate developer contributions to municipal candidates causally increase multifamily housing permitting
- **Citation:** Jennifer Gaudette and Justin de Benedictis-Kessner, "Local Money: Evaluating the Effects of Municipal Campaign Contributions on Housing Policy Outcomes," Harvard Kennedy School / Growth Policy Program working paper, 2024 — https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/local-money-evaluating-effects-municipal-campaign-contributions-housing-policy (full paper PDF: https://scholar.harvard.edu/sites/scholar.harvard.edu/files/jdbk/files/local_money.pdf)
- **Geography:** US-national, multi-city/multi-state municipal elections (not LA-specific, but directly on-topic for "developer/real-estate money in municipal politics")
- **What it finds:** Using a novel dataset of over 3 million municipal campaign contributions across thousands of U.S. cities in five states, the authors find that real-estate industry contributions (median donation $500, vs. $100 for non-real-estate donors) are causally associated with more multifamily housing permits being approved — evidence that local campaign money translates into concrete land-use policy outcomes, an area understudied relative to federal money-in-politics research.
- **Verbatim quote:** "Money in politics is the subject of great debate at every level of government, yet it has principally been studied at the federal level in the US." ... "campaign contributions from organized interests appear to play an important role in dictating policy outcomes at the local level." — Publication abstract, https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/local-money-evaluating-effects-municipal-campaign-contributions-housing-policy

### Finding 4: System-level modeling suggests campaign spending could theoretically flip control of Congress, though that capacity has shrunk in the last decade
- **Citation:** William Minozzi, Gabriel J. Madson, and David A. Siegel, "Can money buy control of Congress?," *PLOS ONE*, 2024 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11207044/
- **Geography:** US-national (US House)
- **What it finds:** Rather than studying single races, the authors aggregate nonlinear, heterogeneous spending effects across all congressional campaigns using a system-level/machine-learning approach. They find that historically observed levels of partisan spending were large enough to have plausibly flipped which party controls the chamber, but this feasibility has declined over roughly the past decade, likely due to widening spending gaps and diminishing marginal returns on additional expenditure.
- **Verbatim quote:** "Can a political party spend enough across electoral campaigns to garner a majority within the U.S. Congress? Prior research on campaign spending minimizes the importance of campaign heterogeneity and fails to aggregate effects across campaigns, rendering it unable to address this question. Instead, we tackle the question with a system-level analysis of campaign expenditures." — Abstract, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11207044/

### Finding 5: Los Angeles's small-donor matching-fund program was substantially less effective at empowering small donors than New York City's — the design details of matching-fund programs matter
- **Citation:** Michael J. Malbin and Michael Parrott, "Small Donor Empowerment Depends on the Details: Comparing Matching Fund Programs in New York and Los Angeles," *The Forum: A Journal of Applied Research in Contemporary Politics*, 15(2), 2017, pp. 219–250, DOI 10.1515/for-2017-0015 — https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/for-2017-0015/html?lang=en (working-paper version: https://scholars.org/sites/scholars/files/malbin_comparing_small_donor_matching_funds_in_new_york_and_los_angeles.pdf; press summary: http://cfinst.org/press/releases_tags/17-08-07/Small_Donor_Empowerment_Depends_on_the_Details.aspx)
- **Geography:** Los Angeles and New York City (direct city-to-city comparison)
- **What it finds:** Comparing the two cities' small-donor matching-fund systems, the authors conclude NYC's program (higher match rates, structured to reward small donors more strongly) increased the number, proportional role, and diversity of small donors in city council races, while LA's program — with a lower match rate at the time studied — did not produce a comparable effect. The paper argues that generic claims about public-financing "success" or "failure" are misleading; specific program design details (match ratios, donation thresholds, eligible donation size) drive outcomes. (Note: LA's match rate was later raised, to as high as 6:1, per 2019 reforms reported by Common Cause California, which is outside the scope of this 2017 paper.)
- **Verbatim quote:** "success" or "failure" will depend both on their goals and the programs' details. — Publication abstract as indexed, https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/for-2017-0015/html?lang=en (I could not render the full PDF text directly — this fragment is drawn from the indexed abstract/press description rather than a page-numbered passage I viewed directly; treat with slightly lower confidence than the other quotes in this report and verify against the primary PDF if precision is required.)
